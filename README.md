# MicaR-EU-regulation
MiCA NotebookLM 
A lightweight GitHub repository for organizing a MiCA / MiCAR learning and research setup based on NotebookLM, with optional companion prompts for Gemini, ChatGPT, and Claude. The goal is to centralize source documents, track key regulatory materials, and make it easier to build AI-assisted workflows for understanding MiCAR, CASPs, stablecoins, white papers, and Luxembourg/CSSF implementation.

Purpose
This repository is intended to support a structured knowledge base around:

Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 on markets in crypto-assets (MiCAR)

CASP authorisation and passporting

ART and EMT topics, including stablecoins

White paper obligations

Luxembourg / CSSF guidance and circulars

ESA technical standards, guidelines, and Q&As

Suggested structure

How to use
Gather official MiCAR, ESMA, EBA, and CSSF materials.

Upload the curated documents into NotebookLM.

Add a custom Gemini Gem or other AI project with tailored instructions.

Use the setup to explore qualification, authorisation, white papers, governance, and supervisory implementation questions.

Keep the knowledge base extensible by adding new RTS, ITS, guidelines, Q&As, and local circulars over time.

Disclaimer
This repository is for learning, research, and internal knowledge management only. It does not constitute legal advice, regulatory advice, compliance sign-off, or an official interpretation of MiCAR, CSSF expectations, or any other legal framework.

Any AI-generated answer based on this material may be incomplete, outdated, context-sensitive, or incorrect. Use this setup at your own risk and only with enterprise-authorised AI tools that are approved by your organisation.

Before using any output for business, licensing, product design, client communications, governance, or regulatory submissions, validate it with qualified Legal, Compliance, Risk, and relevant control functions.

Notes
Prefer official sources first: EUR-Lex, ESMA, EBA, European Commission, CSSF.

Keep commentary sources clearly separated from binding legal texts.

Record document dates and versions whenever possible.

Treat token qualification questions with caution; they often depend on specific facts.
